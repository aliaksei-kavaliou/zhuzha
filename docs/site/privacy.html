Zhuzha — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

Zhuzha is a reading app that compiles study books on your device. It has no server, no account, and no analytics. This policy describes what happens to your data, which is mostly: it stays on your device.

What the developer collects

Nothing. Zhuzha has no backend. The developer operates no server, receives no telemetry, runs no analytics or crash reporting SDK, and serves no advertising. There is no account to create and no way for the developer to identify you or see your books.

The single exception is a content report, which only exists because you chose to send one, and which reaches the developer as an ordinary email from your own mail app. See Reporting content below.

Your API key

Zhuzha requires you to supply your own Google Gemini API key. It is stored encrypted on your device, in the Android Keystore via EncryptedSharedPreferences. It is never written to a plain file, never included in a backup made by the app, and never transmitted anywhere except to Google's own API when generating a book.

What is sent off the device, and to whom

To generate a book, Zhuzha sends text to Google's Gemini API using your API key. The app communicates with no other network service of any kind.

What is sent:

  • The topic, daily reading time and prior-knowledge description you type when creating a course
  • The course outline and previously generated chapters, as context for writing the next chapter
  • A passage you select together with the question you ask about it
  • A record of how a chapter was read — which passages were asked about or marked, and notes you wrote — used to shape later chapters
  • Briefs describing pictures to draw

Because you use your own API key, this traffic is between you and Google under your agreement with Google. Google's handling of it is governed by the Google APIs Terms of Service and the Gemini API terms, not by this policy. Review them if that matters to you: ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms

Nothing is sent to the developer automatically. The one exception is a report you choose to send — see Reporting content below.

What stays on your device

Your books, chapters, pictures, notes, handwriting, questions, bookmarks and reading progress are written as ordinary files in the app's own folder on your device's storage (Android/data/app.zhuzha/files). This is deliberate — they are plain and inspectable — Markdown and JSON — so they remain readable even if this app stops existing.

Reporting content

Every page the app generates can be reported from inside it — this is required of apps that produce AI content, and it is the only feature that sends anything to the developer.

A report is written to a file on your device first, at reports.md beside your books, and you can read it before deciding anything. Sending is a separate, optional step that hands the text to your own email app, addressed to reports@zhuzha.app. The app does not send it; your mail client does, and you can edit or abandon it at that point.

A report contains the passage you are reporting, which output it was (a page, an answer, or a picture), the book and chapter it came from, and anything you choose to write. Declining to send still leaves the record on your device.

Backups you choose to make

If you use the backup feature, Zhuzha writes copies of your books to a folder you pick, using Android's document picker. That folder is yours; the app only writes to the location you grant it. It never uploads anything.

Export

Exporting a book writes an EPUB file to a location you choose. Whether that file leaves your device is entirely your decision. Annotations are excluded from an export unless you explicitly switch them on.

Permissions

Zhuzha requests three permissions, and only the first is used by the app's own code:

  • INTERNET — to reach the Gemini API. This is the only network use, and the Gemini API is the only host contacted.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE and WAKE_LOCK — merged in by the Onyx stylus SDK that provides low-latency handwriting on Boox devices. Zhuzha's own code calls neither.

It requests no access to contacts, location, camera, microphone, phone state, storage, Bluetooth, or your photo library.

Children

Zhuzha is not directed at children and the developer does not knowingly collect data from anyone. Books are generated by a third-party AI model from prompts you write; as with any generative model, output is not guaranteed suitable for children.

Deleting your data

Deleting a book from the Library removes its files. Uninstalling the app removes all of its data from your device, including the stored API key. Backups you made to your own folder are not touched — delete those yourself.
