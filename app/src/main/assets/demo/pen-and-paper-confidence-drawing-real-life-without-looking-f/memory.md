--- Chapter 1: Embracing the Wobble: Why Mistakes Are Just Lines ---
Welcome to the summary of Chapter 1, designed to anchor your memory for the rest of the course. In this opening chapter, titled *Embracing the Wobble: Why Mistakes Are Just Lines*, we established the foundational mindset that perfection is a trap and that your hand is not a machine. We introduced the core concept of the **Zero-Tension Grip**, where you slide your fingers far back from the pen tip to let go of micromanaging every millimeter, trading stiff anxiety for fluid, living motion. We also introduced **Making Marks on Purpose** and the philosophy of the **Human Signature**—the idea that your natural wobbly, overlapping strokes are not errors, but the personal rhythm of a living person leaving a record. 

To help these ideas click, we leaned into specific physical metaphors. We compared gripping a pen too tightly to trying to "squeeze water out of a dry twig," highlighting the physical tension we instinctively bring to the page. Later, to explain how messy, overlapping lines build a drawing, we used the metaphor of a "bird building a nest," where a thrush gathers a bundle of imperfect, bendy twigs until a sturdy shape emerges out of the chaos. 

When it came to terminology, we made deliberate choices. We explicitly chose to banish the word "mistake," replacing it with the neutral framing that "there are only lines" and that a misdirected stroke is simply "new territory." We also used the term "human signature" rather than "style" or "imperfection" to emphasize that your messy lines carry your unique, unedited motion. Finally, we locked in these lessons with two active exercises: a ten-second continuous flowing loop to feel physical release, and a rapid, overlapping ten-circle challenge to practice creating furry, energetic shapes without the safety net of an eraser.

--- Chapter 2: Looking Really Closely at Your Coffee Mug ---
In Chapter 2, we tackle the illusion of memory by looking really closely at a physical coffee mug. We learn that our brain loves to supply a shorthand mental symbol—like a floating circular loop—instead of what is actually there, forcing us to bypass our memory to draw what our eyes truly see. To defeat this mental stencil, we discover that the rim of a mug is actually a squashed, flattened ellipse rather than a round circle. We also introduce the Continuous Line rule, paired with our Zero-Tension Grip from Chapter 1, ensuring we never lift our pen from the paper so we can silence our inner critic. The main metaphor used is a bird building a nest out of messy twigs to describe our meandering, continuous ink path. For terminology, we specifically prefer "compressed oval" over "ellipse" to keep things grounded, and we embrace the "Human Signature" to celebrate messy, overlapping lines rather than mistakes. The exercises guide the learner to first practice capturing the true, squashed shape of the top opening using overlapping, scribbly lines without lifting the pen, and then culminate in a full fifteen-minute live continuous-line drawing of the mug.
